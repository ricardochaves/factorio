# 40-reactor nuclear power plant with solar panels and accumulators

Nuclear power plant with 40 reactors in two columns of 20, 640 heat exchangers and 1,292 steam turbines, with a calculated maximum output of 6,240 MW. It is version 2 of the 40-reactor plant: it does not have version 1's protection circuit, and its fuel inserters have their own network of solar panels and accumulators, separate from the turbines' network; the repository owner recommends it over version 1.

- File: [`nuclear-power-plant-40-reactors-v2.txt`](nuclear-power-plant-40-reactors-v2.txt) — blueprint string; in-game, its name is `Nuclear power plant - 40 reactors v2`.
- Origin: the repository owner's design, adapted from a ready-made design by an unknown author; no license is known.
- This is always the current version of v2; earlier versions of it live in the git history. `nuclear-power-plant-40-reactors-v1`, with a protection circuit, is a different design and stays in the catalog.

## Differences from v1

- No protection circuit: version 2 has neither the decider combinator nor the power switch of version 1, so the base connects directly to a big electric pole of the turbines' network.
- Only one steam pump: version 1 had 66 pumps between the tanks and the turbines, and version 2 has one. The steam runs in two independent halves, west and east, and the single pump links one to the other (see "How the steam is distributed").
- A network of its own for the inserters: the 80 fuel inserters sit in a group of poles apart from the turbines' group, with 21 solar panels and 7 accumulators; version 1 had only 1 solar panel and 2 accumulators, for its combinator.
- Same as version 1: the 40 reactors, the 640 heat exchangers, the 1,292 turbines, the 68 tanks, the 80 inserters, the 44 roboports, the 80 chests, the 64 water inputs and the fuel-saving logic (the inserters' conditions are the same).

## Inputs

- Water: 64 pipes to ground, 32 on the north edge and 32 on the south edge. Connect each one to a water source; at full power the plant needs about 6,430 units of water per second (calculated: 10.3 per second for each of the 624 heat exchangers needed for 6,240 MW).
- Fuel: 40 requester chests ask for 10 uranium fuel cells each; the logistic robots of the 44 roboports bring the cells, and the depleted uranium fuel cells leave through the 40 active provider chests. The blueprint does not include any robots: the logistic network needs logistic robots, a chest with uranium fuel cells and a chest that takes the depleted cells.
- Base: connect the base to one of the 19 big electric poles, which belong to the turbines' group, for example the one at the center, 12 tiles north of the south edge. Do not connect the base to the poles of the fuel inserters or the solar panels: that would put the base's demand on them and undo the separation.
- Start-up: as in version 1, a reactor gets fuel from its chest only after a depleted cell leaves it, so put one uranium fuel cell into each of the 40 reactors manually. This was not tested in this version. The fuel inserters run on solar panels and accumulators, so they move only by day or while the accumulators hold charge.

## How the steam is distributed

In version 1 the steam formed a single network. In version 2 there are two independent halves, west and east, each with 320 heat exchangers, 646 turbines and 34 tanks, all fed by the same water network. The single pump, near the north edge and powered by the solar panels' group, moves steam from the west half to the east half. The 40 fuel-saving thresholds read a tank of the east half; the steam level of the west half is not monitored. The counts were made by a script on the string, and how this arrangement behaves was not measured.

## How the plant saves fuel

In the base game, a reactor with fuel burns nonstop, even when nobody uses the heat. Here each reactor gets a new cell only after its depleted cell is taken out, and the 40 inserters that take out the depleted cells are wired with a green wire to a storage tank in the east column, near the south edge. Each pair of reactors has a threshold: the first is refueled only while that tank holds less than 24,000 units of steam, the next less than 23,000, and so on in steps of 1,000, down to 5,000 for the last pair. That tank must stay in the steam network.

## How the separate network keeps the inserters running

In version 1, the steam pumps and the fuel inserters ran on the plant's own power, so a switch cut off the base when it demanded more than the plant produces. Version 2 has no such switch: the inserters' group of poles has no copper wire to the turbines' group, where the base connects. The inserters' group holds the 80 inserters, 21 solar panels, 7 accumulators, the single pump and 7 of the 44 roboports; the other 37 roboports stay in the turbines' group (counted by a script on the string).

The idea of the design, according to the repository owner, is to keep the whole plant running at maximum output all the time, even if the base starts demanding more than the plant produces. This was not measured in-game. The solar panels produce only by day; at night the inserters depend on the accumulators' charge.

## How it was tested

There was no functional test. The test built the 8,659 entities of the blueprint in the game and connected it to a power source. One of the 2 pole groups was out of the source's reach, so the test joined it with an added wire, which links the two networks only in the test; no machine was fed any items. The catalog validator confirmed that the string is valid and uses only base-game items (version 2.0.77). The image was captured in-game and shows the blueprint built, not running.

## Corrections made here

- The string had no name and no description. Its name is now `Nuclear power plant - 40 reactors v2`, and the description, in English, gives the calculated power and consumption, the inputs, how to connect the base and how to start it.
- The original string is the one the repository owner provided; the rest of the decoded content is identical to it, checked by script.

## Known limitations

- Nothing was measured in-game in this version: the delivered power, the consumption, the flow of steam with a single pump between the two halves, the start-up and how it behaves under a base that demands more than the plant produces.
- The 40 fuel-saving thresholds read a tank of the east half, and the single pump, powered by the solar panels' group, is the only link between the two halves; the steam level of the west half is not monitored, and this was not tested.
- The inserters' network depends on solar panels and accumulators; at night or with the accumulators empty, the inserters stop, and its behavior in those conditions was not tested.
- The 7 roboports of the inserters' group idle at 350 kW (7 × 50 kW), and the 21 solar panels give at most 1,260 kW (21 × 60 kW), by day only (game values, calculated): charging robots at those roboports can starve the inserters, and this was not tested.
- The 37 roboports of the turbines' group share the power shortage with the base if the base demands more than the plant produces.
- The logistic robots were not tested.
- If the requester chests run out of cells, the reactors are no longer refueled; the logic is that of version 1, but this was not tested in this version. Keep a stock of cells in the logistic network.
- The edges use plain hazard concrete, not refined hazard concrete.
